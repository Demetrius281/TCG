Frequently Asked Questions About Bali Property Laws & Regulations.

Can a foreigner own a villa in Bali?+

Foreigners cannot directly own Hak Milik (freehold) land in Indonesia. Depending on eligibility, lawful alternatives include Hak Pakai, a lease or ownership through an eligible PT PMA structure. Holding property rights and operating a rental business are separate legal matters.

Explore Foreign Ownership Regulations →
My land is held by an Indonesian nominee. What is the risk?+

A nominee arrangement can put your investment at risk. The Indonesian certificate holder remains the registered owner, while agreements designed to give a foreigner effective freehold ownership may be legally invalid. Bali’s Perda No. 4/2026 introduces additional restrictions. The consequences and available restructuring options depend on the actual documents and circumstances.

Understand Nominee Ownership Risks →
What does a villa need to rent legally?+

Legal rental operations require more than an Airbnb listing or NIB. Land rights, zoning, building approvals, SLF, business licensing and applicable tourism requirements must align with the intended activity. The exact requirements depend on the property, its approved use and the operating structure.

View the Legal Rental Checklist →
My villa is listed on Airbnb. Is it legal?+

Not necessarily. An active listing does not establish that the property is authorised for short-term rentals. The building’s approved use, zoning and operator licences must meet the applicable requirements. Authorities are increasing compliance checks, including cooperation with booking platforms.

Explore Rental Regulations & Enforcement →
Can I rent short-term in a residential zone?+

Not automatically. Residential zoning does not necessarily permit commercial accommodation. The applicable RDTR and its ITBX provisions determine whether the intended activity is allowed, conditional or prohibited on a specific parcel. Kuta Selatan’s confirmed RDTR is Perbup Badung No. 59/2021, covering 2021–2041.

Understand Bali Zoning Rules →
The building is larger than its approved PBG. What happens?+

Construction that differs from approved plans can delay or prevent SLF issuance and may trigger enforcement measures. Some discrepancies can be addressed through revised approvals, while others require physical alterations. The outcome depends on zoning limits, technical requirements and the extent of the deviation.

Explore PBG & SLF Requirements →
Is my old IMB still valid?+

Possibly. An IMB issued before the PBG system may remain valid for a completed building that has not been materially altered. Extensions, changes of function or differences from approved plans require further assessment. An IMB does not automatically replace an SLF.

Understand Legacy IMB Rules →
Can I set up a PT PMA to run my villa?+

Potentially, depending on the activity and current investment restrictions. Bali has restricted new foreign investment registrations in certain business categories. Owning property, operating accommodation and managing another owner’s villa may involve different KBLI classifications and licensing requirements. Eligibility must be confirmed through OSS.

Review PT PMA Restrictions →
What tax applies to rental income?+

Qualifying short-term accommodation services in Badung generally fall under the regional PBJT framework, with a 10% rate for hotel services. National income taxes apply separately according to the operator’s circumstances. Long-term residential leasing and other arrangements may follow different tax rules.

Explore Rental Tax Requirements →
I live abroad. Do I need to be in Bali?+

Not necessarily. Initial document reviews, consultations and written assessments can be handled remotely. If a procedure requires your presence, notarised documents or a power of attorney, TCG can identify the requirements and help coordinate the process where legally permitted.

What if my property cannot be legalised?+

We’ll tell you clearly. If the intended use cannot be approved, the audit explains why and evaluates alternatives such as redesign, another permitted use, project recovery or sale. The goal is to protect the remaining value of your investment rather than recommend further spending without a sound legal and financial basis.

Answers reflect regulations in force on 28 October 2026. Each property is assessed against the current rules for its location.
