03 / Investigation

What We Check Before Recommending a Solution.

01

Land Rights & Ownership

Land certificate (SHM, HGB or Hak Pakai), lease terms and the registered holder, including any nominee arrangement affecting control, operation or sale.

02

Zoning & Permitted Use

The applicable RTRW and RDTR, the parcel’s subzone and activity rules, and any existing KKPR, measured against the intended use.

03

Building Approvals & Compliance

PBG or a qualifying legacy IMB, the approved function and drawings compared with the as-built condition, and the SLF status.

Business & Rental Licensing

NIB, the KBLI 2025 code for the activity actually carried out, OSS obligations and applicable tourism standards.

05

Operating Structure

The operator’s identity and contractual role, whether owner, PT PMA or licensed management company, and its fit with the licences held.

06

Regulatory Exposure & Feasibility

Official notices, 2026 provincial restrictions and enforcement risk, with each issue classified as correctable, authority-dependent or a potential blocker.

07 / FAQ

Questions Owners Ask Before an Audit.

Can TCG make my villa legal for short-term rentals?+

Short-term rental requires the intended activity to be compatible with the parcel’s zoning, the building’s approved function and SLF, the operating structure and the business licences held. The audit establishes whether a lawful corrective route exists and what it involves. TCG can coordinate implementation under a separate engagement. Approval decisions rest with the competent authorities.

NIB, OSS and Villa KBLI →
What if zoning prohibits short-term rentals?+

A business licence, an NIB or a third-party management company cannot override a land use that the applicable RDTR prohibits on that parcel. Where accommodation is excluded, the assessment examines the uses the zoning does permit and whether formal spatial confirmation is needed where the position is uncertain. Licensing is pursued only once the spatial position supports it.

Zoning, RDTR and KKPR →
Can an existing building obtain PBG and SLF after construction?+

Official SIMBG guidance provides an existing-building route that can issue PBG and SLF together once the applicable requirements are met. Feasibility depends on spatial compatibility, building position, intensity limits, structural and technical compliance, and available documentation. Each building is assessed individually, and approval is not automatic.

How Existing Properties Are Legalised →
Is an IMB issued before 2021 still relevant?+

An IMB issued before PP No. 16/2021 can remain valid for a building completed and kept in its originally approved configuration. Later extensions, alterations or a change of function may require PBG and further assessment. The audit checks the IMB date, construction status, approved function and drawings against the building as it stands today.

PBG, SLF and Legacy IMB →
Can a PT PMA operate or manage a rental villa?+

Eligibility depends on the company’s registered activity and KBLI 2025 code, its structure, current OSS licensing, applicable investment restrictions and the permitted use of the property itself. Since May 2026, OSS in Bali has been closed to new PT PMA registrations in 18 business categories, including hotel and other accommodation categories. Availability for villa activity is confirmed live in OSS.

PT PMA and Foreign Owners →
Can TCG resolve nominee ownership risks?+

TCG reviews the land documents, agreements and the registered holder’s position, and coordinates qualified legal advice on lawful alternatives, such as a lease or an eligible title held in the investor’s or company’s own name. Perda Bali No. 4/2026 prohibits nominee land ownership. Restructuring options and their enforceability depend on the documents and the parties involved.

What if the property has received an official warning?+

The notice is reviewed first: the issuing authority, its legal basis, the violations cited, the corrections required and the response deadlines. The assessment then identifies which issues can be addressed and coordinates qualified legal or technical advice where needed. Enforcement continues during an audit, so the deadlines in the notice still apply.

Does the audit include obtaining permits?+

No. The audit establishes the findings and the corrective action plan. Permit applications, technical works, government charges, specialist fees and other implementation work are scoped and priced separately, whether carried out by the owner’s own advisers or through a separate engagement with TCG.

What documents are needed for the initial review?+

The property location, the land certificate or lease, and any available PBG or IMB are enough to begin. SLF, approved drawings, business licences, operator agreements and official notices can be added where available. Incomplete records are common, and identifying the gaps is part of the assessment.

How long does the audit take?+

Standard audits target five business days after the agreement is signed, full payment is received and complete documentation is available. Government response times, additional specialist investigations and corrective implementation fall outside this target. Complex cases, or those requiring site inspection or confirmation from an authority, are scheduled individually after the initial review.

Can the assessment be managed remotely?+

Yes. Document review, consultations and report delivery can be handled remotely for owners outside Indonesia. Site inspections, local verification and any procedures with the relevant Bali authorities are coordinated on the ground where the agreed scope requires them.

What if the intended property use cannot be made compliant?+

The report states the restriction, its basis and the alternatives that remain feasible, such as a different permitted use, a revised operating arrangement or preparing the property for sale with its legal position documented. Licensing expenditure is recommended only where it can achieve a lawful outcome.
